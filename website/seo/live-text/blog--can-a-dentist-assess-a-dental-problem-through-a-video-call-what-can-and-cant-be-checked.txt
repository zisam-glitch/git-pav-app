Can a Dentist Assess a Dental Problem Through a Video Call? What Can and Can't Be Checked
By Dr Bhojani
November 12, 2026

Having a toothache, sore gum or broken tooth can leave you wondering if you should visit the dental clinic. In some cases, it can be useful to have a video call with a dentist. This lets you describe your symptoms and show them the area which is causing you problems.

Dentists can definitely help you with some dental issues during a video call. They will ask questions to determine what you should do next. However, a smartphone camera isn't the same as a physical examination so they may not be able to help with all dental issues.

Can a Dentist Check Your Teeth Through a Video Call?

Yes, they can examine any visible issues on camera. They may want you to open your mouth wide, move the phone closer or show the area from different angles.

They will also ask questions about your symptoms, such as when the pain started, if it is getting worse and if there is any swelling or sensitivity.

This will allow them to give you an initial assessment and determine if you require any advice, an in person appointment or urgent treatment. However this may not be a definitive diagnosis, particularly if the issue is within the tooth or under the gum.

What Dental Problems Can Be Assessed Online?

A video consultation can be useful if you are experiencing any of the following issues, especially if there are visible symptoms of your issue.

1. Toothache and Sensitivity

Your dentist may want to know where your toothache is. They may also ask what makes it worse. For example, do you have any pain when eating, drinking cold things or biting?

This gives your dentist an idea of what could be causing the issue. However, they will not be able to confirm what the problem may be until they have looked at your teeth properly.

2. Swollen or Bleeding Gums

You can point out the areas that are red, swollen or sore. The dentist might also ask if your gums bleed when you brush them or if you have noticed any changes to your gums around a specific tooth.

This could indicate gum inflammation or infection and an examination is usually required to assess the gums further.

3. Chipped or Broken Teeth

Visible chips/ cracks may easily be seen on camera. The dentist will then examine the affected area and ask questions about how the damage happened.

Questions may also be asked about if it causes any pain/sensitivity to the tooth. If it's a severe injury then you will likely be assessed by a dentist to see how much of the tooth has been affected.

4. Swelling Around the Mouth or Jaw

If you have any puffiness around your jaw, cheek or mouth, they may be able to see this on the phone. They can ask when it started and if it is getting worse.

This helps them decide if you need to come in for an examination. If there is severe or worsening swelling, especially if you are having trouble breathing or swallowing, then this should be seen by a medical professional immediately.

What Can't a Dentist Check Through a Video Call?

There are some limitations to this. You will only ever be able to see the visible areas of your mouth as the camera does not give you the same information as having instruments or x-rays.

1. Hidden Tooth Decay

Hidden damage between teeth or under the surface would not show up on camera. If you have symptoms which suggest a cavity, your dentist will not be able to see how large or deep it is on a video call.

2. Problems Below the Gum Line

Gum disease affects the supporting tissues and bone around your teeth. The dentist will not be able to accurately measure gum pockets or assess how well your teeth are supported by looking at them on a screen.

A physical examination is required.

3. Problems Inside the Tooth

Some dental issues will affect the nerve of the tooth or area around the roots. This may mean you'll need x-rays or other tests.

You can't see what is going on inside your tooth or underneath the jaw on a video call.

4. Loose Teeth and Bite Problems

A dentist might be able to spot a displaced looking tooth but will not be able to properly assess how loose it is or how your upper and lower teeth fit together when you chew.

These tests need to be done in-person.

When Are Virtual Dental Solutions Helpful?

Virtual dental solutions can help provide general guidance. You can talk to a dentist, describe your symptoms and see if you need to come into the clinic.

This can be useful if you have minor tooth pain, a chipped tooth, sore gums or have any queries after a recent dental treatment. The dentist can advise on the next steps via a video consultation.

This can also be a good way to discuss treatments or ask further questions. Find out more about our virtual dental solutions and how video consultations can help you with your dental care.

How to Prepare for a Video Dental Appointment

To make the consultation as smooth as possible, you could do some basic preparation beforehand. Find a quiet well lit place to have your call and ensure your camera and microphone are working.

It also helps:

Be prepared by noting when the issue started and if there have been any changes.

Note any pain, soreness, swelling or bleeding.

Keep details of any recent dental treatments and medications.

Have a small mirror to hand.

Try to keep your phone still whilst showing your teeth. If the dentist is unable to get a clear view they may ask you to adjust the camera angle or send secure photos.

When Should You Visit a Dentist in Person?

Video appointments are not appropriate for all dental issues. If you are experiencing any persistent pain, suspected infection, a loose tooth or damage then you should book an in-person appointment.

Treatment such as fillings, root canal, tooth extraction and repair of broken teeth will need an in person appointment.

If you have severe swelling, trouble breathing or swallowing or heavy bleeding then you should NOT wait for a video appointment as this requires urgent medical attention.

Final Thoughts

You may find it helpful to have a video call with your dentist. They can help provide clarity on any dental issues you are experiencing by looking at them on screen. This is helpful if your issue is something you can clearly describe.

However, they still won’t be able to physically examine you with equipment and x-rays. Your dentist will advise you to come in-person to see them if they need to take a closer look.

FAQs
Can dentists help me with my toothache via video call?

The dentist will be able to ask you questions and look at the area; however, an in-person examination may be necessary.

Can dentists see cavities on video call?

You may be able to see some cavities but any hidden decay between your teeth would need an in person assessment.

Can I show my swollen gums to my dentist?

Yes you can show your dentist the area and they can give you some initial advice. However you may need to be assessed closer.

Can I have my teeth checked via video call?

No, regular check ups are needed so they can assess your teeth and gums properly and spot any problems.

Are video consultations useful for dental emergencies?

They can help you with initial advice but if there is severe swelling, trouble breathing or swallowing, or heavy bleeding then you should seek immediate medical attention.
